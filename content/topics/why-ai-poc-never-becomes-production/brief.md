# Post Brief

**Title**: Why Your AI Proof-of-Concept Never Makes It to Production — And How to Scope One That Will

**Target keyword**: why AI proof of concept fails to reach production

**Audience**: Founders and technical leads at small-to-mid-size companies (2–250 people) who have already run — or paid for — an AI pilot/PoC/demo that impressed everyone in the room and then quietly died before shipping. Bottom-funnel: they've felt this exact pain and are now vetting whether a new vendor or approach will avoid it, which is a project-risk concern, not a curiosity question.

**Angle**: PoCs die in production for a structural reason, not a technical one: a PoC is scoped to prove the model *can* work on a curated example, while production requires proving it works reliably across the messy, adversarial long tail of real inputs — and almost nobody scopes the PoC to test that gap on purpose. The fix isn't "build a better PoC," it's changing what the PoC is scoped to prove: not "can this work" (a demo answers that) but "where does this break, and how bad is it when it does" (only a deliberately adversarial evaluation answers that). This reframes "project risk" concretely instead of abstractly, which is what a buyer burned by a dead PoC actually wants to hear.

**Supporting points**:
- Describe the common PoC failure pattern: a curated demo with clean inputs and a friendly audience "proves" feasibility, gets internal buy-in, then hits production data that's messier than anything tested — and nobody budgeted time to find that out before committing to a timeline.
- Name the real gap: a PoC answers "can this work at all," production requires answering "how often does this fail, on what kinds of inputs, and what's the cost of failure" — a completely different, harder question that most PoC scopes never ask.
- Give the reader a concrete reframe: before greenlighting a PoC-to-production jump, ask "what's our evaluation set of hard/edge-case inputs, and what failure rate is actually acceptable for this use case?" — if nobody can answer this, the PoC hasn't actually de-risked anything.
- Contrast a PoC scoped to impress vs. a PoC scoped to find failure modes early — the second is less flashy in the room but is the one that actually predicts whether production will work.
- Position discovery-phase evaluation (testing architecture against real, messy data before committing to a fixed-scope build) as the direct fix — tie back to Jasper AI's model without naming it as a sales pitch first.
