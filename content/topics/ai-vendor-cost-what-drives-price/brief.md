# Post Brief

**Title**: What a Custom AI Project Actually Costs — And Why Two "Same Scope" Quotes Can Differ 3x

**Target keyword**: how much does a custom AI project cost

**Audience**: Founders and technical leads at small-to-mid-size companies (2–250 people) who have gotten, or are about to get, quotes for a custom AI agent, RAG system, or LLM application, and are confused why prices vary wildly for what sounds like the same request. This is bottom-funnel: they are actively pricing, not learning what AI is.

**Angle**: Price variance in AI project quotes isn't vendors padding margin — it's vendors pricing different amounts of *unresolved risk* into the same line item. A quote that assumes the first RAG architecture will work is cheaper and more likely to blow its budget; a quote that reserves time to test and pivot the architecture is pricier upfront and more likely to actually ship. The real cost driver isn't features, it's how much architecture uncertainty the price implicitly resolves. Frame cost as a function of unknowns tested, not hours billed — this is a direct, defensible expression of Jasper AI's discovery-then-fixed-scope model.

**Supporting points**:
- Break down what actually drives cost in a custom AI build: integration complexity, data readiness (is there clean, accessible data or does it need to be built), architecture risk (RAG vs. fine-tune vs. agent vs. knowledge graph), and ongoing eval/maintenance — not "how many features."
- Explain why a quote that looks cheaper often excludes the discovery/architecture-validation step — meaning the client pays for it later anyway, just disguised as scope creep or a failed v1.
- Give a rough, honest range framing (e.g., a scoped discovery engagement vs. a full fixed-scope build) without overcommitting to specific numbers that will age badly — describe the shape of pricing, not a rate card.
- Contrast "cost of building it right the first time" vs. "cost of a cheap build that needs a second build" — most failed AI projects cost more in the end, not less.
- One concrete question a buyer should ask any vendor: "Does this quote include testing whether the architecture fits the use case, or does it assume it already does?"
