# Post Brief

**Title**: 7 Questions to Ask an AI Agency Before You Sign — Most Won't Have Good Answers

**Target keyword**: questions to ask before hiring an AI development agency

**Audience**: Founders and technical leads at small-to-mid-size companies (2–250 people) actively comparing AI vendors/agencies — they have at least one proposal in hand or are taking sales calls this week. Bottom-funnel, vendor-selection stage.

**Angle**: Most vendor-comparison content is generic ("check their portfolio," "ask for references") because it's written by marketers, not people who've actually scoped these projects. A buyer evaluating an AI vendor needs technical, specific questions that expose whether the vendor understands architecture risk — the thing that actually determines whether the project ships. Frame this as a practical checklist a non-technical founder can use in a sales call, with each question explained so they understand *why* the answer matters, not just what to ask.

**Supporting points**:
- Q1: "What's the one architecture decision this project hinges on, and how will we know by week two if it's wrong?" — exposes whether they've actually thought about fit, or are just pricing a feature list.
- Q2: "Is architecture validation a separate, paid step, or is it bundled into the fixed-price build?" — reveals whether risk is priced honestly or hidden.
- Q3: "Who owns this project day-to-day — will I be talking to the person who's actually building it?" — surfaces the sales/engineering handoff problem that inflates scope and slows communication.
- Q4: "What happens if the data isn't clean enough for the first approach — is that in scope or a change order?" — the most common real-world cause of AI project overruns.
- Q5: "Can you show me a case where you recommended NOT building something, or changed architecture mid-project?" — a vendor who's never said no or pivoted hasn't been tested by a hard use case.
- Q6: "How do you define 'good enough' for this use case, and who signs off on it?" — exposes whether success criteria exist before the build starts.
- Q7: "What's your plan for this after launch — who maintains/evals it, and what does that cost?" — many agencies quote build cost only and go silent on the maintenance burden.
- Close with: the pattern across all seven questions is the same — does this vendor treat architecture fit as something to be tested, or something to be assumed?
